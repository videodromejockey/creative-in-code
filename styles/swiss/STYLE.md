# Swiss style

**Summary:** The International Typographic Style of 1950s–60s Switzerland: a grid, sans-serif type, asymmetry, empty space and flush-left text.
**Sub-styles:** Basel, Zürich
**Reference images:** `<work folder>/_research/swiss/images/`, outside the skill. They are not in this repo. `corpus/SOURCES.md` says where to find them.

## Principles

Be rooted in the style, and experiment inside it. The grids and treatments in this file are examples from earlier work. They are not a closed list. A new treatment is good if it follows the principles below and has a mathematical, objective feel.

The corpus gives five features of the style:

1. **A modular grid.** Every element aligns to it. Josef Müller-Brockmann's *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* is the classic text.
2. **Simple sans-serif type.** Akzidenz-Grotesk, Helvetica and Univers are the typical typefaces.
3. **Asymmetry.** Do not center the layout.
4. **Empty space.** Use space to balance the composition.
5. **Flush-left text.** Align text left, with a ragged right edge.

The style wants to look universal, anonymous and objective. It gives information clearly and does not decorate.

Both schools come from one teacher, Ernst Keller, at the Kunstgewerbeschule Zürich (1918–56).

## Sub-styles

### Zürich

Designers: Josef Müller-Brockmann, Richard Paul Lohse, Carlo Vivarelli, Hans Neuburg, Ernst Hiestand, Nelly Rudin. The journal *Neue Grafik* (1958–65) carried the Zürich view.

Traits:

- Strict adherence to the grid. The grid itself is open to invention: the column count, the modules and the proportions can change. Everything must align to it, and it must feel mathematical.
- Less photography. Geometric shapes and type carry the design.
- More reliance on prominent text.
- Preference for Akzidenz-Grotesk.
- An objective structure: information in a clear order, one accent colour.

Checklist:

- [ ] Each element aligns to a grid line or a module edge. You can state the grid in numbers.
- [ ] All text is flush left, in a sans-serif typeface.
- [ ] The layout uses one accent colour at most, plus black, white or paper.
- [ ] The shapes are geometric and measured, not freehand.
- [ ] The grid lines are not visible in the final piece, unless the design is about them.

### Basel

Designers: Emil Ruder, Armin Hofmann, Karl Gerstner, Hermann Eidenbenz, Fridolin Müller, Robert Büchler, Ruth Pfalzberger, Markus Kutter. They taught or studied at the Allgemeine Gewerbeschule Basel.

Traits:

- Looser adherence to the grid.
- High-contrast photography.
- Lettering placed like art: very large, cropped, rotated or layered.
- Experiment with Univers (Ruder).
- Layers and overprints. Image treatments such as halftone, threshold and cut-out.

Checklist:

- [ ] The type or the image is large enough to be the main shape.
- [ ] The photo treatment has high contrast and reads at a distance.
- [ ] The layers stay legible where they overlap. The copy is still easy to read.
- [ ] The layout is free, but it still has an order (a baseline, an axis, a margin).

## Grid and format

Choose any strict grid that suits the format. Examples:

- **Earlier poster sets:** 905 × 1280 units, a margin of 56, 6 columns, a gutter of 18. They were rendered at 2x, for screens. The default is now print quality (see `references/craft-notes.md`).
- **Other options:** a square module grid, or a grid from the proportions of the photo.
- **A grid from a ratio:** for example the golden ratio. See the Bennett essay in `corpus/SOURCES.md`.

Write the grid as constants at the top of the script, so that all positions come from it.

## Type

- **Typefaces of the style:** Akzidenz-Grotesk (Zürich), Univers (Basel, Ruder), Helvetica.
- **Substitutes:** use a free or installed grotesque or neo-grotesque sans, for example Helvetica or Arial on macOS. Check which fonts are installed before you choose. If you need another font, follow `references/font-sources.md`.
- **Brand fonts:** if the client has a design system, use its fonts and keep the Swiss layout.
- Use large size contrasts: a very large title and small text for the information.
- Use a small number of weights.

## Colour

- Black, white or off-white paper, plus one strong accent. Red is typical.
- A Basel piece can overprint two or three colours, like a riso or a silkscreen print.
- If the client has brand colours, use them as the accent.

## Image treatments

These treatments worked in earlier projects. They are starting points only. Invent new ones.

- The photo unedited, cropped hard to the grid.
- Halftone and dot screens.
- Riso or silkscreen overprint in two colours.
- Threshold and bitmap series.
- Woodcut or engraving hatching.
- Contour lines and ridge lines (with hidden lines removed).
- Circle packing and triangle mosaics.
- A traced vector silhouette of the subject.
- Chronophotography: repeated positions in one frame.
- A measured plate: the subject with dimensions, angles and a key.
- A specimen drawing, as in a natural history plate.

When the brief says the background is not relevant, make a mask or a trace of the subject and drop the background.

## Do and do not

- Do: align every element to the grid.
- Do: keep the copy clear and complete.
- Do: use empty space.
- Do: make your own images from the supplied photo.
- Do not: center the layout by default.
- Do not: use a photo from a reference website in the final output.
- Do not: use a serif or display face for the main text, unless a brand requires it.
- Do not: fill the page with texture or noise. Clean shapes read better.

## Review checklist

- [ ] The layout follows a grid that you can state in numbers.
- [ ] The text is flush left and does not run past its column.
- [ ] No letters are clipped, unless a Basel title is cropped on purpose. Ask the user if that is not clear.
- [ ] No text collides with another element.
- [ ] The piece is not too heavy at one end.
- [ ] Each piece in a set uses a different treatment.
- [ ] The subject of the image is clear. It does not read as a different object.
- [ ] The copy is correct (dates, times, place, URL).
